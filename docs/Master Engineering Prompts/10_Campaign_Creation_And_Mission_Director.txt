A complete campaign workflow
A creator should be able to:

Create a campaign project.
Choose a source edition and target runtime.
Design or import a BSP.
Place scenery, vehicles, weapons and actors.
Define player insertion and mission objectives.
Build AI encounters and patrol/migration paths.
Connect trigger volumes and events visually.
Configure doors, checkpoints and supported level transitions.
Add dialogue, music, effects and cutscenes.
Compile standard native tags and HaloScript.
Launch the mission in a compatible engine.
Return to the same editable source.

Important engine restrictions
Stock Halo 1 can use multiple scenario BSPs, but referencing multiple BSPs is not equivalent to seamless simultaneous world streaming.

Treat level transitions, collision boundaries, spawn visibility and object ownership correctly.

Campaign co-op and player count must be validated separately for each runtime.

Do not promise six-player campaign support merely because an engine supports large multiplayer lobbies.

Native completeness
Validate AI firing positions after collision edits, scenario/detail palettes, native script budgets, sound and animation references, chapter transitions and missing source assets.

Flagship acceptance
Finish First Contact as an actual short campaign mission with insertion, exploration, scripted interaction, combat encounter, objective completion, cinematic or extraction and successful end state.

Verify a full human playthrough.

Then test cooperative play only on a runtime that explicitly supports the required features.

One complete, playable mission is the acceptance standard—not a populated scenario that merely opens in Sapien.
